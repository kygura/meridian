# Coastal home: where to buy a villa ($1–2M)

A place to live, not a trip: a house or villa on a remote beach coast with mild, comfortable weather,
in Southeast Asia or Central/South America, for US$1–2M. Researched 20 November 2026 from sources dated 2024–2026.
Prices, visa thresholds and listings move, so re-check each before acting on it.

Who it's for: one buyer with Spanish and German passports, in his twenties, working remotely.
That matters twice: Panama's Friendly Nations visa covers both passports, and the Philippines' retirement visa
(SRRV, now from age 40) is closed to him.

## Ranking

| # | Coast | Why | Main tradeoff | Main legal risk | Nearest city in Meridian |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Todos Santos / Pescadero**, Baja California Sur, Mexico | The mildest climate on the list: Nov–Jun highs 25–29°C, lows 12–17°C, dry. Small town, 1–1.5 h from Los Cabos (SJD) | Aug–Oct is hot, muggy and in the hurricane path (Odile, 2014). Healthcare is clinics; hospitals in La Paz and Cabo | No direct title within 50 km of the coast: you hold it through a 50-year renewable bank trust (fideicomiso). Avoid unconverted ejido land; check water supply | `la-paz-mexico` |
| 2 | **Nosara**, Nicoya Peninsula, Costa Rica | Remote but properly liveable: a settled expat town, surf, fibre in parts. Foreigners hold titled land with just a passport | Hot (32–35°C highs), humid in the green season (May–Nov). Liberia (LIR) is 2.5–3 h, the last part unpaved | First 50 m above high tide is public and the next 150 m is concession land; much beachfront sits in the Ostional refuge. Liens, surveys and water letters | `liberia-costa-rica` |
| 3 | **Bali, Bukit/Uluwatu**, or **South Lombok** for the remote version, Indonesia | The mildest coast in Southeast Asia: trade winds, highs 30–31°C, July lows ~21°C, no typhoons, dry Apr–Oct. Best healthcare in the region (Bali) | The Bukit is no longer remote. Lombok is, with thinner services and fewer flights. Dec–Mar is wet | Freehold (SHM) is Indonesian only. A foreigner holds Hak Pakai (30+20+30 years) or a 25–30-year lease. Villas advertised as "freehold" need converting; nominee deals are illegal | `denpasar-indonesia`, `mataram-indonesia` |
| 4 | **Pedasí / Azuero**, Panama | The quietest and easiest place to settle legally: Friendly Nations residency (Spain and Germany qualify) with US$200k of titled property. US dollar economy, outside the hurricane belt | Hot all year (32–33°C dry-season highs). PTY is 4.5–5 h by car; hospitals 40–60 min away in Las Tablas and Chitré | Much beachfront is ROP (rights of possession), which gives weak protection and can't be mortgaged. Buy titled *finca* only | `chitr-panama` |
| 5 | **Langkawi**, Malaysia | The cleanest ownership in Asia: real freehold for foreigners above RM1M with state consent. MM2H residency is open from age 25. Very safe | The hottest and most humid coast here (31–34°C). Villas at US$1M+ are scarce | Much of the landed stock is Malay Reserve land, which foreigners can't hold. Some sellers offer "buy the company's shares" instead, a nominee-style workaround to avoid | `alor-setar-malaysia` (closest listed) |

**Pick:** Todos Santos if "mild" comes first; Nosara if you want outright title and a ready community.
Bali/Lombok is the Asian answer, accepting Hak Pakai instead of freehold.

**The thing to plan around:** on every coast above, the strip right on the beach is the weakest title
(Mexico's federal zone and trust, Costa Rica's concession zone, Panama's ROP, Indonesia's freehold ban).
Look for titled land set back from the sand, a short walk to the beach.

## Looked at and left out

- **Trancoso, Brazil:** the best tropical climate (24–29°C) and a lovely town, but faction violence in neighbouring Caraíva (12 killed in police operations in 2025, curfews), marine-land and rural-land title rules, and little stock between US$1M and 2M. Worth a second look if the security picture improves.
- **José Ignacio, Uruguay:** the safest and legally simplest, but winters run 8–16°C with wind and grey skies, so not mild all year. From 2026 the tax holiday needs ~US$2M of real estate.
- **Olón, Ecuador:** pleasant climate and cheap, but the UK advises against all but essential travel to Santa Elena, and a cartel killing happened inside a gated community there in July 2026.
- **Koh Samui / Khao Lak, Thailand:** liveable with a deep villa market, but only a 30-year lease is certain, longer leases were never enacted, and since 2026 the government raids villas held through nominee companies.
- **Siargao / El Nido, Philippines:** beautiful and remote, but typhoons, weak healthcare, 25+25-year leases only, and almost nothing at US$1–2M.
- **Vietnam:** a September 2026 draft would take landed houses out of foreign ownership altogether.

## Before buying

- **Title first:** a lawyer of your own (not the seller's) checks title type, liens, zoning and permits before any deposit. Every coast above has a common trap: ejido land, ROP, concession land, Malay Reserve, "freehold" in Indonesia.
- **Rent first:** a season in the top two, in the bad months (Todos Santos in September, Nosara in October), shows the heat, humidity and storms the listings don't.
- **Water and power:** Baja and Guanacaste both refuse or ration water connections; ask for the letter.
- **Taxes:** check where you'd become tax resident and how Spain treats the move before you buy.
