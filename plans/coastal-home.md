# Coastal home: where to buy a villa ($1–2M)

A place to live, not a trip: a house or villa on a beach coast for US$1–2M, in Southeast Asia,
Central/South America or New Zealand. The United States is out.
Researched 20 November 2026 from sources dated 2024–2026. Prices, visa thresholds and listings move,
so re-check each before acting on it.

**What counts, in order:** close to nature (parks, forest, reefs, trails), close to an international airport
(about an hour or less), real infrastructure (a private hospital, fibre), mild weather, and a title a foreigner can hold.
Remoteness for its own sake is no longer a goal: a quiet street near a national park beats a village three hours from a runway.

Who it's for: one buyer with Spanish and German passports, in his twenties, working remotely.

**Pick: Florianópolis.** It's the only place that hits all five: nature on every side, an airport 15–35 minutes away with a direct
flight to Lisbon, real hospitals, weather that stays mild in both seasons, and freehold title. A R$1M (~US$190k) purchase also gives
a 4-year Brazilian residence permit that asks for just 14 days in the country every two years.

**Runner-up: Guanacaste near Liberia**, if you'd rather have the tropics. Hotter, but titled land, a nonstop to Zürich and
national parks within the hour.

**The thing to plan around:** the most beautiful plots sit right on the protected land. In Florianópolis that means dunes,
restinga and lagoon margins (APP) plus federal coastal land; in Costa Rica the 200 m maritime zone. Buy a registered urban lot
a short walk from the protected area, and have a lawyer check the environmental status before anything else.

## New Zealand: not buyable on this budget

A non-resident can't buy an existing home in New Zealand. Since 6 March 2026 there's one exception: holders of the
Active Investor Plus visa can buy one home worth NZ$5M or more. That visa itself needs NZ$5M invested for 3 years,
so the route costs about NZ$10M, and land next to the foreshore is excluded even then. The other way in is to become
resident first (a skilled job with an accredited NZ employer, or buying into an NZ business) and live there 12 months.
EU passports get no shortcut; only Australians and Singaporeans do.

If that changes, the coasts that fit the brief are the Matakana–Omaha coast (marine reserves, ~1 h 15 from Auckland)
and Sumner on Banks Peninsula (~30 min from Christchurch). Bay of Islands and Nelson are lovely but have only domestic airports.

## Looked at and left out

- **Nosara and the Southern Pacific of Costa Rica (Uvita, Ojochal):** the best jungle-meets-ocean in the region, but 2.5–4 h from an international airport, and the planned southern airport has no date.
- **Pedasí, Panama:** easy Friendly Nations residency for both passports, but 4.5–5 h from Panama City's airport and thin healthcare.
- **South Lombok:** wild bays 30–50 min from the airport, but only clinics nearby and one or two international routes.
- **Langkawi:** freehold is possible, but it's the hottest coast, villas in budget are scarce, and the hospital is public.
- **Trancoso, Brazil:** lovely and mild, but faction violence next door in Caraíva and little stock in budget.
- **José Ignacio, Uruguay:** safe and legally simple, but winters of 8–16°C.
- **Olón, Ecuador:** the UK advises against all but essential travel to Santa Elena.
- **Thailand:** a 30-year lease is the only certain title, and villas held through nominee companies are being raided in 2026.
- **Philippines and Vietnam:** no land for foreigners; Vietnam's September 2026 draft would end foreign ownership of houses.

## Before buying

- **Title first:** a lawyer of your own checks title type, liens, zoning, permits and environmental status before any deposit.
  Brazil has no escrow or title insurance, so in Florianópolis that means the *matrícula*, federal coastal-land status (SPU), APP mapping and the *habite-se*.
- **Rent first:** a season in the top two, including the worst months (Florianópolis in a winter storm, Guanacaste in October), shows what listings don't.
- **Taxes:** check where you'd become tax resident and how Spain treats the move before you buy.
