# Coastal home: where to buy a villa ($1–2M)

A place to live, not a trip: a house or villa on a beach coast for US$1–2M, in Southeast Asia,
Central/South America or New Zealand. The United States is out.
Researched 20–21 November 2026 from sources dated 2024–2026. Prices, visa thresholds and listings move,
so re-check each before acting on it.

**What counts:** mellow surf within about 20 minutes (forgiving sand-bottom beach breaks and soft points, not heavy reef),
nature close by (parks, forest, reefs, trails), an international airport within about an hour, real infrastructure
(a private hospital, fibre), mild weather, and a title a foreigner can hold.

**Pick: Florianópolis.** It's the only place that hits everything: a soft beach break 10 minutes from home, nature on every side,
an airport 15–45 minutes away with a direct flight to Lisbon, real hospitals, mild weather in both seasons and freehold title.
A R$1M (~US$190k) purchase also gives a 4-year Brazilian residence permit that asks for just 14 days in the country every two years.
The cost is a wetsuit from June to September.

**Runner-up: Punta de Mita / San Pancho**, for warm water and the mellowest waves on the list. The trade-offs are owning through a
bank trust, a hot rainy summer, and cartel noise around Jalisco in 2026.

**The thing to plan around:** the most beautiful plots sit right on protected or restricted land. In Florianópolis that means dunes,
restinga and lagoon margins (APP) plus federal coastal land; in Mexico the federal beach zone and ejido land; in Costa Rica the 200 m
maritime zone. Buy a registered lot a short walk from the beach, and have a lawyer check the title and environmental status first.

## New Zealand: not buyable on this budget

A non-resident can't buy an existing home in New Zealand. Since 6 March 2026 there's one exception: holders of the
Active Investor Plus visa can buy one home worth NZ$5M or more. That visa itself needs NZ$5M invested for 3 years,
so the route costs about NZ$10M, and land next to the foreshore is excluded even then. The other way in is to become
resident first (a skilled job with an accredited NZ employer, or buying into an NZ business) and live there 12 months.
EU passports get no shortcut; only Australians and Singaporeans do.

If that changes, the coasts that fit are the Matakana–Omaha coast (marine reserves, ~1 h 15 from Auckland)
and Sumner on Banks Peninsula (~30 min from Christchurch).

## Looked at and left out

- **Penang, Malaysia:** freehold, the best hospitals and a national park, but no surf.
- **Bali's Bukit / Uluwatu:** almost all reef, and heavy. Not chill.
- **Guanacaste's Playa Hermosa / Papagayo:** 30 min from the airport, but almost no surf.
- **El Salvador's Surf City (El Sunzal, El Zonte):** a direct flight to Madrid since September 2026 and US Level 1, but the breaks are over rock and reef, coastal titles are often unregistered, and Canada warns that foreigners can be detained under the ongoing state of exception.
- **Nosara and the Southern Pacific of Costa Rica:** Playa Guiones is the most forgiving beach break in Costa Rica, but 2.5–4 h from an international airport.
- **Pedasí, Panama:** easy residency, but 4.5–5 h from the airport and thin healthcare.
- **Langkawi:** hot, scarce stock, public hospital only, no surf.
- **Trancoso, Brazil:** faction violence next door in Caraíva.
- **José Ignacio, Uruguay:** winters of 8–16°C.
- **Olón, Ecuador:** the UK advises against all but essential travel to Santa Elena.
- **Thailand:** a 30-year lease is the only certain title, and villas held through nominee companies are being raided in 2026.
- **Philippines and Vietnam:** no land for foreigners; Vietnam's September 2026 draft would end foreign ownership of houses.

## Before buying

- **Title first:** a lawyer of your own checks title type, liens, zoning, permits and environmental status before any deposit.
  Brazil has no escrow or title insurance, so in Florianópolis that means the *matrícula*, federal coastal-land status (SPU), APP mapping and the *habite-se*.
- **Rent first:** a season in the top two, including the worst months (Florianópolis in July, Nayarit in September), shows the water temperature, crowds and storms that listings don't.
- **Taxes:** check where you'd become tax resident and how Spain treats the move before you buy.
