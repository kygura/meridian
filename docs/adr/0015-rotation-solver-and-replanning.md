# A beam-search solver proposes rotations, and a daily check proposes plan B

Builds on ADR 0011 (Rotation), ADR 0013 (real place data), ADR 0014 (air quality and normals) and ADR 0016 (notifier and daily jobs). Uses the extension point ADR 0011 left for a solver.

The owner wants Meridian to propose where to live for the next 12–18 months from a pool of places, explain why, and keep watching once the plan is under way. Two parts: a **Rotation Solver** that is pure and deterministic, and a **re-plan loop** that only ever proposes.

**The engine** (`src/lib/solver/engine.ts`, pure, shared by server and browser). Input: candidates (pool places plus the places of fixed stays), each with twelve month readings (the owner's Personal Windows first, else the Comfort Index, as `stayFit` does), a cost, internet and healthcare figure with its source or null, a coast distance, a region and risks; fixed stays (locked, booked, where the owner is on the start day); past presence (the rotation's committed stays and the journal's journeys) for day counting; constraints, preferences, weights and a seed. The horizon is cut into half-month slots, and further at every fixed stay's edges so a fixed stay is a run of whole slots and keeps its exact dates. A beam search walks the slots in order: each state either stays put (up to the place's maximum nights) or moves to another pool base (once the current stay has its minimum; a run squeezed between fixed stays may be shorter). The beam keeps the best 96 states per slot by the weighted score so far. Ties break on a hash of the seed and the path, so the same input gives the same output with no randomness. Distinct proposals come from the final beam, then from re-runs that penalise slots already used, and a proposal must differ from every accepted one in a fifth of its nights. Cost is O(I·S·W·P): at most 3 × results runs, 24–40 slots, width 96, at most 40 pool places, under two million cheap steps; an 18-month, 30-place solve takes well under a second (timing test). Beam search is not exhaustive and can miss a plan whose payoff comes many slots later; we accept that for speed and explainability.

**Hard constraints** are checked on every step, so an illegal state is never kept, and every returned plan is checked again by `evaluatePlan`, the single source of truth, which drops anything that fails: the horizon covered with no gap or overlap; fixed stays exactly where they are; minimum and maximum nights; Schengen 90 days in any 180 (entry and exit days count, past presence included; on by default, off for EU, EEA and Swiss passports); the owner's country rules (days per entry, days in a window); internet and healthcare floors where measured (unmeasured is allowed and listed); months the owner avoids; excluded places. Meridian has no visa database: Schengen is the one built-in rule (with its source), and every other limit is the owner's, with their note. The property tests run 24 seeded random pools, locks and rules and assert that every proposal is legal, covers the horizon and keeps its locks.

**The score** is the weighted mean of six components, each 0–100 and each shown with the figures behind it. *Fit*: night-weighted month fit (great 100, good 80, fair 55, poor 25, avoid 0; unknown 50 and named). *Cost*: 100 at or under the monthly budget, falling to 0 at 1.5 × budget, from the owner's own figure or the place-data estimate (ADR 0013, labelled with source, granularity and year); unknown is 50 and named, and left out of the average shown. *Travel*: each move costs 1 point plus 0.3 per hour plus 1 per $200, from `legs.buildLeg` (the trip legs' stated estimates, not fares), scaled to a year. *Visa margin*: full marks with 15 or more spare Schengen days and 30 or more nights under the tax threshold (the common 183-day test, configurable, stated as a general rule). *Risk*: curated advisories and conflicts, seasonal hazards within 1,000 km in their months, and, when asked, the place's news Issues and Watch Flags, whose weight fades over the horizon because news describes now. *Preferences*: fuzzy wishes with weights: "somewhere in a region, these months" (sub-regions by UN M49 country lists, the broad world regions, or one country), "by the sea, these months" (from a new coast-distance dataset, `data/city-coast.json`, built from Natural Earth's 1:10m coastline), and "at most N long-haul flights a quarter". Each preference reports the share it met.

**Buffers, border runs and hubs.** When a stay outside Schengen follows a Schengen stay and a Schengen place would have broken 90/180 there, the stay is labelled a Schengen buffer with the days used. With border runs allowed, a stay that would pass a per-entry limit is split by two nights in the nearest pool place in another country outside Schengen, and says why. A pool pass-through that lies on a long move (7 h or more, at most 15% detour) is inserted as a hub, taking its nights from the next stay, and says how it splits the move.

**Alternatives.** For every stay the solver placed, every other pool base is tried on the same dates; legal ones are re-scored and the best three are listed with the score difference and the two components that moved most. A swap in the UI re-scores the edited plan on the server; locking a stay re-solves with it pinned.

**The API and screen.** `/api/solver` (owner-only, ADR 0009): settings (pool, budget, horizon, rules, preferences, weights, re-plan thresholds) in one `solver.json` document in the shared store, so JSON-file and Postgres both work; `solve`, `evaluate` and `accept`. The Rotation screen gets a Solve mode: the pool from the rotation, the shortlist, past journeys or search; sliders for weights; proposals side by side on one timeline and on the map, each with its breakdown, locks and swaps. Accepting writes the plan's stays as **planned** with `origin: solver`, and deletes only the unlocked, unbooked, not-current stays it replaces, after a confirm that lists them. ADR 0011 said proposals land as ideas; we changed that for an explicit accept, since the owner chose this plan as the active one, while the Solve flow itself still writes nothing. Ask gets `solve_rotation`, owner-only and read-only, which returns each option with its reasons.

**The re-plan loop** (`server/lib/replan.ts`, registered as the daily job "replan"; logic in `src/lib/solver/replanLogic.ts`). Each day it watches the current stay and the next one to four (Settings) for **Shifts**: news Issues and Watch Flags with a headline in the last 14 days (older issues are standing risk, already in the solver's score; flags count one level stricter than issues, and the default is patterns only, because nearly every large place has some flag), curated advisories added in the last 60 days, PM2.5 over the last 7 days (Open-Meteo air quality) against the month's normal, mean temperature (ERA5 via `observedWeather.ts`) against normal, the fare for the next move against the first fare seen for it (only with Duffel or SerpAPI; never mock prices), and Schengen or country-rule breaches after an edit. Each shift has a magnitude, its distance past the threshold. Continuous readings fire at 1 + h and clear below 1 − h (h, the hysteresis, defaults to 20%); discrete ones fire at 1 and clear only after being absent for the cooldown; and a shift that raised a proposal cannot raise another within the cooldown (7 days). A source that cannot be reached is reported as not checked and never read as a clearing. When something fires, the loop re-solves from today with the place penalised (news 30 points, air 40, weather 25, fare rise 20) or excluded (a new curated advisory of severity 3) and everything booked, locked or current held fixed, and stores a **Proposal**: the shifts, their evidence with sources and links, plan B, and plan B as a diff against the active rotation with score, monthly cost, Schengen peak and travel before and after. Gaps in the active rotation are said, since its "before" figures cover only the nights it holds. A new proposal supersedes older open ones. The owner accepts, dismisses or snoozes it; accept refuses a plan B whose fixed stays have since changed. Proposals and book-by reminders go through the notifier with stable dedupe keys (`replan:<id>`, `book-by:<stay>:<date>:<kind>`), so they appear in the Alerts list and on every channel once.

Rejected: an ILP or exhaustive search (exact but slow and opaque for 40 slots × 40 places with window-based day counting, and it would need a solver dependency); month-only slots (too coarse for pass-throughs and for Schengen margins); a DP keyed on the full day-count state (the Schengen window makes the state space explode); writing proposals straight into the rotation (the owner must decide); counting every existing news issue as a shift (it raised fifteen on the first real run); treating unknown data as safe or as zero; a built-in visa table (no source we can cite and keep current; ADR 0012's module can feed `countryRules` later); and mock fares as a shift source.
