# A Rotation plans where the owner lives; it is not a Trip

Leaves ADR 0007 and ADR 0010 as they are: a Trip is still a Season out of a home airport and back.

The owner does not take trips from a home. They live in a rotation of bases held for weeks or months, moving when the season changes, with short pass-throughs (a hub such as Bangkok) between them. A Trip cannot say that: it starts and ends at a departure (LPA by default), it is anchored to months so it can be taken any year (ADR 0007), nights per stop exist only once a year is chosen (ADR 0010), and its legs include the way home. Stretching it would mean a trip with no home leg, dates required up front, and months-long "stops", which breaks both ADRs for every other trip. So a **Rotation** is its own thing, and the two meet only through conversions.

**Model** (`src/lib/rotation.ts`, pure, shared by server and browser). One rotation per owner: settings (name, an optional home airport that is never a route end, and a horizon of 12–18 months that rolls with today), **Rotation Stays** and **Place Profiles**. A stay has a place (city id, name, country, airport, coordinates), a type (`base` or `pass-through`), a status (`idea`, `planned`, `booked`, `current`, `done`), a move-in and move-out day *or* a month window (`YYYY-MM` to `YYYY-MM`) before dates are fixed, minimum and maximum nights, a monthly budget and the actual monthly rent (amount and currency as entered, never converted, as in ADR 0010), lodging in the trip stop's shape, notes, a `locked` flag, an optional lead-time override and an `origin`. Dates are calendar days; nights are `end − start`, never stored; one stay's move-out day may be the next one's move-in day. "Current" and "done" are also derived from today (`effectiveStatus`), so a planned stay becomes current without anyone editing it, while an idea stays an idea. The owner's **current location** is the committed stay containing today (on a moving day, the one moved into); ideas never count.

**Gaps and overlaps** (`rotationIssues`) are found between committed stays in calendar order, plus stays outside their own minimum or maximum. Ideas are options and are left out. A gap or overlap involving a month-window stay is marked tentative, and two month windows sharing a month are not an overlap, since that is what windows look like before dates are fixed. Issues are shown, never refused: the API rejects only contradictions inside one stay (moving out before moving in, a minimum above the maximum).

**Personal Windows override the Comfort Index.** Per place the owner records peak, shoulder and avoid months with a reason ("burning season, air quality"). Fit (`stayFit`) reads each month of a stay from the owner's window first and the Comfort Index (ADR 0002) only where there is none: peak is great, shoulder fair, avoid avoid; comfort bins fold into great/good/fair/poor. A stay's level is the night-weighted mean of its months, except that a week or more (or over a quarter of the stay) in an avoided month makes the whole stay "avoid". The sheet shows every month, its nights and why, so the rule is visible, not hidden in a colour. With neither source the fit is "unknown"; nothing is guessed.

**Booking lead times.** Each stay still to book (an idea or planned stay that has not started) gets a book-by date: the move-in day (or the window's first day) minus the longer end of its lead time. The default is a stated rule of thumb by type and length (pass-through 2–3 weeks; a base under four weeks 3–4; one to three months 6–8; longer 10–12), shown as such. A place profile overrides it (Azores 8–10 weeks, safari lodges 52) and a stay overrides the place. Deadlines show on the timeline and as a list with monthly-rental searches: Flatio (city only; its URL takes no dates), Airbnb with the dates (monthly pricing from 28 nights) and Booking.com with the dates, built by `bookLinks.stayLinks` so only the city, country and dates leave the app. `dueReminders(state, today, noticeDays)` returns what a daily job should send (overdue, or due within the notice period), and `rotationRemindersDue()` in `server/lib/rotation.ts` reads the store and calls it; `GET /api/rotation/reminders` exposes the same. Sending, and not sending twice (stay id plus book-by date is a stable key), is the notifier's job. Meridian books nothing.

**Moves and totals.** A move is a leg between consecutive committed stays in different cities, built with `legs.buildLeg`, so flight time and cost, ground modes from landmass, and the stated estimate method are exactly the trip legs' (ADR 0010). There is no return leg. Totals over the horizon: months covered (union of nights, so overlaps count once; window stays included and dated months shown apart), cost per month per currency (night-weighted rent where entered, else budget, labelled), moves and moves per year, and the estimated travel cost in USD with the legs' method.

**Storage and sync.** `server/lib/rotation.ts` keeps one `rotation.json` document in the shared store, so it works on the JSON-file and Postgres backends (ADR 0006). `/api/rotation` is owner-only (ADR 0009), gated inside its router. Unlike trips, the browser keeps no copy: the screen reads the server on open and on window focus, and writes one stay or place profile at a time with the `updatedAt` it was edited from. If another browser saved that item since, the server answers 409 with the current copy and the screen reloads instead of overwriting; deleting leaves a tombstone so an edit to a stay deleted elsewhere is refused too. This is per item, so edits to different stays from two browsers never collide.

**Conversions.** `tripToRotation` turns a Trip's placed stops into stays in route order: dated stops keep their dates; stops with months get a month window in the trip's year (months before the season's start in the next year, as in ADR 0007), or the next time those months come round; a stop with a stay booking reference is booked, a confirmed trip's stops planned, the rest ideas; the trip's departure becomes the optional home airport and the way home is dropped. `journeyStayTemplate` turns a stop of a past Journey into a template: the place, the type and length it really had (as minimum and maximum nights), and, when the journal recorded the lodging cost, that cost scaled to a month as the budget, with the arithmetic written in the notes. The template has no dates; the owner places it.

**Extension points.**
- *Visa and day counting* (built separately, keyed by country and date ranges): `stayPresence(stays)` returns `{ stayId, country, start, end, tentative }` for committed stays, with window-only stays flagged tentative. `RotationPlace.country` is filled from the city dataset. The visa module needs nothing else from the rotation and the rotation imports nothing from it.
- *Solver* (proposes whole rotations from a pool of places): `solverInput(state, today)` gives the locked stays, the pool (`PlaceProfile[]`) and the horizon. A solver returns stays with `origin: { kind: "solver" }` and status `idea`; `rotationIssues` is its hard constraint, `stayFit` and the lead times its objective, and it must not move a `locked` stay. Proposals land as ideas, which never count as commitments until the owner promotes them.
- *Notion importer* (the owner's own windows): `PersonalWindow.source = { kind: "notion", ref }` and `mergeImportedWindows(profile, windows, "notion")`, which replaces that source's windows and keeps hand-written ones, so a re-import updates rather than duplicates. It writes through `PUT /api/rotation/places/:cityId` like the screen.

Rejected: a Trip flag ("rotation mode") or a trip with no home leg (it would undo ADR 0007's reusable seasons and ADR 0010's itinerary rules for every trip); several rotations per owner (one life, one calendar; scenarios belong to the solver's proposals as ideas); keeping the rotation in the browser with the trips' three-way merge (it is one owner's private plan with no offline use case worth the complexity, and per-item conflict refusal is simpler to reason about); a whole-document PUT (two browsers would overwrite each other's stays); storing nights or "current" (derived, two sources of truth); market rent figures (no real source; only the owner's rent, budget or own journal); and windows keyed by country (the owner's reasons, such as air quality in Chiang Mai, are local; a country default can be added beside the city key later).
