# Entry rules come from an open dataset and a sourced curated file; one engine counts every day limit

Fills the place ADR 0011 left for a visa module and replaces ADR 0015's "Meridian has no visa database": the solver's Schengen check and its owner-typed country limits now run through this engine, with the owner's rules kept as overrides.

The owner lives in a rotation across countries, on one or more passports. Whether a plan is legal depends on what those passports may do in each country (visa free for how long, on arrival, an ETA or e-visa, a visa first, no entry), on how the days are counted (per entry, in a rolling window, pooled across the Schengen Area), on whether the passport is valid long enough and on yellow-fever certificates. Separately, days present decide tax residency. Before this, Meridian knew one rule (Schengen 90/180, switched on because the passport was unknown) and whatever limits the owner typed.

**Data.** `scripts/build-visa-data.ts` (`npm run build:visa`) builds `data/visa-requirements.json` from Passport Index Data (github.com/imorte/passport-index-data, MIT, scraped by its maintainer from passportindex.org), pinned to one commit and recorded with the dataset's own date (17 February 2026) and the fetch date: 199 passports by 199 destinations, one compact token per pair (`vf90`, `voa30`, `eta`, `ev`, `vr`, `na`). The older ilyankou dataset is archived since January 2025, so the successor it points to is used. The same script reads the UK FCDO's travel advice through the GOV.UK content API (Open Government Licence) and keeps each destination's "Passport validity requirements" paragraph word for word, with its link and last update; a rule ("6 months after you arrive", "3 months after you leave", "valid for the stay") is read from it only when the wording is one of the plain forms (188 of 196 pages), and the rest stay as quotes. FCDO writes for British passports; validity rules are the destination's and usually the same for every passport, which the screens say. Overseas territories (French Guiana, Réunion, Aruba, Gibraltar…) are not folded into their state: they have their own rules, and the dataset has no row for them, so they read "no data".

`data/visa-curated.json` is written by hand. Every row has a source link and the date it was checked, and only rows that could be checked against an official page are in it:

- *Zones.* The Schengen Area's 29 members (European Commission page, Bulgaria and Romania in full since 1 January 2025), 90 days in any 180 with entry and exit days counted (Regulation (EU) 2016/399, Art. 6), free movement for EU, EEA and Swiss passports, and Cyprus and Ireland named as EU members outside it.
- *Regimes* (10): Thailand's visa-exempt stay cut to 30 days for arrivals from 15 September 2026 with land entries capped at 2 a calendar year (the dataset, from February, still says 60); Vietnam per entry; Indonesia's 30-day visa on arrival, extendable once; Georgia's year; Albania, Montenegro, Turkey and Cyprus at 90 in any 180; UK visitors at 6 months a visit. Most cite FCDO pages, which state the rule for British passports; the curated row applies the window to every passport the dataset lists as visa free for the same days, and says so.
- *Digital-nomad visas* (5): Spain (200% of the minimum wage, €2,442 a month for 2026), Portugal (four times the minimum wage, no euro figure on the official page, so none here), Thailand's DTV (500,000 THB savings, 180 days a stay), Sri Lanka (USD 2,000 a month remitted) and Croatia (€3,622.50 a month), each from the issuing government's page. Colombia's was asked for and left out: cancilleria.gov.co refused every fetch, and third-party figures disagree.
- *Yellow fever:* WHO's International Travel and Health Annex 1 (18 November 2022, revised 3 January 2023, still the list WHO links), 125 rows transcribed by hand: whether WHO lists a risk of transmission, and whether a certificate is asked of all travellers or of those arriving from risk countries, with the youngest age.
- *Tax tests* (4): the UK's 183 days in the tax year from 6 April (HMRC), Portugal's more than 183 days in any 12 months (IRS Code Art. 16), New Zealand's more than 183 days in any 12 months, parts of days counted (IRD), Sri Lanka's 183 in 12 months (its nomad-visa terms). Elsewhere a stated general rule: 183 days present in a calendar year, labelled as a rule of thumb.
- *Border-run notes* (4): Thailand's land-entry cap, Vietnam's e-visa entry points, Indonesia's single-entry VoA, the UK's "frequent or successive visits".

**Engine** (`src/lib/visa`, pure, shared by server and browser). Input: passports and a list of country plus date range; the journal's past stops, dated trip stops and Rotation Stays all reduce to that. A stay from `start` to `end` is present on every day from start to end inclusive, the rule border counts use, so a moving day counts in both countries and, in one country, once. `entryRule` picks the easiest passport (own country, free movement, visa free with the most days, on arrival, ETA, e-visa, visa, no entry) and reads the curated file before the dataset; dated rows apply by arrival day. A dataset figure with no curated row is counted per entry, and the screens say that assumption. `countLimits` is the counting core: rolling windows (first day over, peak), per-entry limits (stays that share a day join one entry; a night abroad breaks it) and calendar-year limits. `checkStays(data, passports, stays)` returns a verdict per stay (requirement, days allowed given what came before against days planned, the day it goes over, passport validity against the owner's expiry if given, yellow fever after a stay in a risk country) and the plan's violations. The Schengen helpers give days used and left on any day, the longest stay that fits from a day (planned presence after it included; binary search, since fitting is monotonic), the first overstay and the earliest re-entry. `taxDays` counts days present per country per calendar year, tax year or rolling 12 months against each sourced test, warning 30 days before it; the owner's legal-residency country is shown apart and left out of the warnings. `borderRunOptions` lists the nearest places, one per country, the passports can enter on arrival, with the restrictions noted, and says plainly when a run cannot help (a rolling window). `schengenBuffers` lists places outside Schengen the passports can enter for the nights needed, one per country, ranked by distance and by the Comfort Index in those months, Cyprus and Ireland included. Microstates enclosed by Schengen (Andorra, Monaco, San Marino, the Vatican) and places under a severity-3 curated advisory are never suggested; that is a judgement, not data.

**Settings and API.** Passports held (with an optional expiry), the legal-residency country and countries to watch for tax days are one `visa.json` document in the shared store. `/api/visa` is owner-only, gated by prefix and inside its router; the browser receives only its passports' rows of the matrix, not the whole file. Nothing about the owner leaves the server: every check runs locally against committed files.

**Surfaces.** Plan › Days: the Schengen window as a chart of days used per day (past from the journal, future from trips and the rotation), the four figures that matter today, a date checker, country counters, tax counters and the list of stays counted. Trips: an Entry and visas card in the editor and a badge on each stop (`tripRisk.tripEntryChecks`). Rotation: a badge per stay, the verdict in the stay's sheet, and the plan's violations with buffers or border runs on request (`rotationVisa`). Ask: `check_visa`. Every block links its sources with their dates and says that rules change and this is not legal or tax advice.

**Solver and re-planning.** `evaluatePlan`'s day checks are now `countLimits` over past presence and the plan: one implementation for the solver, Plan › Days and the re-plan loop. The beam still checks each step incrementally for speed, and every returned plan is re-checked by the engine and dropped if it fails, as before. With a passport set, `visaConstraints` turns Schengen on or off by the passport, gives every pool country its limit for that passport as a hard rule (origin "visa"), leaves out countries that refuse the passport, notes stays needing a visa first (legal to plan, the owner may get one), and lets the owner's typed rules replace the data for their country. The service adds the two best Schengen buffers to the pool when no base outside Schengen is within 4,000 km of the pool's Schengen places, and the nearest arrival-friendly place as a border-run stop when runs are allowed; each addition is named in the notes. Border runs only go where the passport enters on arrival. Each proposal lists what was checked (passports, dataset date, limits applied, overrides, gaps, and what is never checked). Without a passport, everything works as before and says so. When the fixed stays alone already break a limit, the error names it instead of "no legal rotation". Determinism is unchanged: the visa constraints are data in the input. The property tests add 12 seeded runs with passports in which `checkStays`, independently of the solver, must find no violation in any proposal, and assert that most trials solve and some use border runs.

Rejected: Timatic or a paid visa API (licence, cost, and the owner's passport would leave the server); scraping government pages per check (slow, fragile, and the owner's plans would hit third parties); filling gaps with a guess (an unknown destination reads "no data", never visa free); treating dataset days as a rolling window (most are per entry, and a wrong window would hide a breach of the other kind; the assumption is stated); one limit for all passports in a country (dual nationals differ); calendar-year limits in the beam (no data row needs one; Plan › Days counts them); dropping the owner's rules when a passport is set (an extension or a newer rule is exactly what they know better); and suggesting buffers without regard to advisories.
