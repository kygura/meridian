# Events are fetched live, and mean festivities, eclipses, seasonal highlights and seasons

Builds on ADR 0007 (trips are seasons, which surface events), ADR 0013 (measured visitor nights), ADR 0016 (daily jobs) and the document store of ADR 0006.

Until now events were `data/events.json`: 44 entries written by hand (six eclipses, Tết and Lunar New Year, a few dozen festivals and races). It went stale the day it was written, covered what one person remembered, and put a fact about the world into the codebase. The owner's rule is that events have to be live and dynamically fetched, not hardcoded. The first version of this ADR replaced the file with Wikidata festivals and sporting events. The first real fetch showed the wrong mix: 159 of 319 events were film festivals and 81 were sports.

The owner then said what they mean: "By events I mostly mean local festivities, eclipses, tourism seasonality, and unique events specific to the location (for example, cherry blossom season in Japan)." This ADR now covers that. No list of events or dates is in the codebase. What the code does hold is definitions: which Wikidata classes to include or leave out, which latitude counts as aurora country, and the rules that find a place's seasons in data.

The cherry blossom example then went wrong in the other direction. The second version of this ADR read the Japan Meteorological Agency's tables for Japan and kept a hand-picked list of species (jacaranda, lavender, tulips, whales and so on) for iNaturalist. The owner's correction: "The Japan cherry blossom event was only an example, not a feature to add on its own. Don't add it as a Japan-specific event. Use it as a precedent and apply the same approach to every country in the world." And about the list: what is notable at a place must come from data, not from species someone chose. This ADR now does that: one rule for every city, with no country source and no species named in the code.

## What was dropped, and why

Film, music, theatre, arts, literature, comics and pride festivals, conferences, and all sports (recurring and dated) are gone, with their queries, fixtures and tests. They are what Wikidata has most of, not what the owner plans trips around. The dated sporting query was also the slow one (18 to 45 s). Dated festival items had come back empty. A stored copy from the first version may still hold such events: a feed that no source produces is dropped when a source is saved, and the read path filters those feeds too.

The Japan Meteorological Agency source (`jma`) and the hand-picked phenomenon list (`server/lib/events/phenomena.ts`) were removed in the same way, with their fixtures and tests. JMA covered one country with a per-country page parser and a name match on Japanese city names, so it could not be applied to the rest of the world, and a species list says what someone remembered to add. A stored `jma` feed is dropped on read and on the next save; nature events written by the phenomenon list (ids `inat-<phenomenon>-<city>`) are dropped on read, and the first highlights check replaces them.

### festivities

Instances of festival (Q132241, subclasses included) with a month of the year (P2922) and at least 5 sitelinks, minus festival classes the owner does not mean: film, music, theatre, arts, literature, dance, comedy, poetry, photography, pride, queer culture, sculpture, science, storytelling, puppet, arts and crafts, comic conventions, ancient Roman festivals, conferences, anime and gaming conventions. The classes are Wikidata QIDs in `server/lib/events/wikidata.ts`, checked against the live labels. The first query excludes by class per type, which keeps it near 1 to 2 s. An item can carry a plain "festival" type next to a film or sport type, so a second query asks Wikidata which of the candidates are of an excluded class or of sport (sporting event, marathon). A backstop regex drops titles and types that name film, music, pride, books, a race or awards. Items with an end date (P576) are skipped, which covers most ancient festivals; those without coordinates fall out at placing.

An item with several months (Oktoberfest) is one event with `months: [9, 10]`. Each is placed on the nearest city within 40 km, the bigger of two cities within 5 km winning; one with no city is dropped and counted. A first run gave 58 events from 83 candidates, with 8 excluded and 17 unplaced.

### national-festivities

Items with a country (P17), a recurrence, at least 20 sitelinks, no coordinates and no venue, in a country that still exists. The recurrence is a month of the year (P2922) or a day in the year (P837). Wikidata gives Holi, Diwali, Songkran, Day of the Dead, Lunar New Year, Tết, Chuseok and the like no month of their own, so P837 is read:

- a Gregorian month, or "April 13", is a recurring month;
- a Hindu lunar month name ("Ashvin Shukla Pratipada") is mapped to the Gregorian month its bright fortnight usually falls in. The lunar month drifts about a month a year, so these events say the month is approximate;
- a Chinese-calendar day ("15th day of the 8th month", "Lunar/Lunisolar New Year's Day") is resolved to Gregorian dates with the runtime's ICU Chinese calendar for the next 18 months, and becomes dated events (a day can be off when the new moon is near midnight);
- anything else (Easter-relative, Thai lunar, Islamic) is counted as undated and left out.

The owner asked to drop items listed in more than about 8 countries. Diwali lists 11 and is one of the festivals asked for, Nowruz lists 23 and is not, so the limit is 12. Types must name a festival, carnival, religious holiday or new year, or be a bare holiday or annual event with 30 sitelinks; titles with "Day" or "Eve", independence days, national days, races, prizes and the like are dropped. The filter still lets a few observances through (Feast of the Annunciation, St John's Eve). Each festival is placed on the country's capital and its 3 biggest airport cities from the city dataset, at least 30 km apart. A first run gave 35 events from 239 candidates and 36 kept, 2 undated and 1 in no known country.

### eclipses

Solar eclipses (Q3887, subclasses included, next 30 months) are placed as before: on airport cities within 150 km of the point of greatest eclipse and on the hub city of each country Wikidata lists. Total lunar eclipses (Q19693318, next 36 months) are kind `sky`, tagged `lunar-eclipse`, and go on the hub cities of the countries Wikidata lists, if any. Wikidata files most lunar eclipses under the plain class, so few qualify: the first run found one, June 2029, with no city. An eclipse with no city is kept and shows in lists, not on the map. Partial and penumbral eclipses are left out.

### inaturalist

The seasonal highlights of a city: the species whose observations near it bunch into a short season, found from the observations themselves. No species, country or phenomenon is named in the code (`highlights.ts` holds the rules, `inaturalist.ts` the client and the rolling source).

1. **Observations by month.** For a city, `GET /v1/observations/species_counts` within 30 km, once per month of the year (`month=1` to `12`), for plants (`iconic_taxa=Plantae`, `quality_grade=any`, 100 rows a page) and for conspicuous animals (`iconic_taxa=Aves,Mammalia,Insecta,Reptilia,Amphibia,Actinopterygii,Mollusca`, `verifiable=true`, 150 rows). That is 24 requests and gives every listed species' count in every month.
2. **Seasonal.** A species is a highlight of the city when at least 60% of its local observations fall in at most 3 consecutive months (wrapping the year) and it has at least 20 observations (plants) or 30 (animals). Peak months are that window, with an edge month dropped when it holds under 15% of the window and the rest still reaches 60%. A month a full page did not list is filled with half of the page's last count (a page that is not full lists everything).
3. **Recognisable.** Taxa below species rank and taxa with fewer than 1,000 observations on iNaturalist as a whole (`observations_count` of the taxon record in the answer) are dropped. The 15 best per kind by count times concentration are stored.
4. **Special here.** The store keeps a tally of how many checked cities have each species among their stored highlights. A city's highlights are ranked by local count x concentration x ln(1 + cities checked / cities with the species), a species that is a highlight in more than 15% of the checked cities is dropped once 40 cities are checked, and at most 3 events are emitted per city. Plants and animals are ranked against their own kind (each score is divided by the best of its kind in the city): iNaturalist holds many times more animal observations, and raw scores left no plant in any list. The ranking is computed from the stored highlights whenever events are built, so a species that turns out to be everywhere disappears from cities checked earlier.
5. **Event.** Kind `nature`, recurring, `months` the peak window. Plants: "X in bloom". Animals: "X season", and for a bird with at least 95% of its observations inside the window, "X migration". X is iNaturalist's `preferred_common_name`, else the scientific name. The note gives the share and the count ("83% of 171 iNaturalist observations within 30 km fall in Mar to Apr."), `sourceUrl` is the matching iNaturalist observations search, tags are the iconic group (`bloom`, `birds`, `mammals`, `insects`, `reptiles`, `amphibians`, `fish`, `marine-life`), `wildlife` for animals and a kebab of the common name.

**Month pages or histograms.** The other way is one `species_counts` for the year and one `observations/histogram` (`interval=month_of_year`) per candidate: 2 + 20 requests for the top ten of each kind, against 24 for the month pages. Measured on the live API, the month pages cost 24 requests in about 24.4 s a city (21 cities, 23.6 to 26.0 s); the histogram route would be 22 requests, about the same. The deciding difference is which species get looked at. Candidates by whole-year count are the commonest species: in Tokyo the flowering cherries have 21 verifiable annotated observations a year against 470 for rose evening primrose, and are never among the top ten. The month pages put a species that is large only in its own months among the candidates, and need no second pass.

**Parameters changed after checking the live API.** The first design asked for `verifiable=true` and the flowering annotation (`term_id=12&term_value_id=13`) for plants. Near Pretoria that leaves 11 flowering observations of jacaranda in October (23 with every grade); with every grade and no annotation it is 153. Near Tokyo the annotated verifiable cherries are 5, 4 and 1 observations at research grade and 21 at verifiable. Planted trees are casual-grade on iNaturalist and are rarely annotated, and planted trees are most of what people travel to see in bloom. Plants therefore use `quality_grade=any` and no annotation. The cost is that "in bloom" is inferred from when a plant is photographed, so a plant mostly photographed for its autumn leaves or fruit would be titled wrongly; I did not read every title in the 48 cities checked. Animals keep `verifiable=true`, which leaves out zoos.

**Coverage.** Every city is eligible. Order: the owner's places (Atlas wishlist, shortlist, rotation, trips: `places.ts`), then every city with measured attention (`cityIdsByAttention()`), then the remaining airport cities by population. A city is re-checked after 60 days; never-checked cities come first, then the oldest. A run checks as many cities as its time allows and at most 60. A run that finds cities still due records `pending` in its status detail. While `pending` is above zero and the last run succeeded, the source counts as having pending work: `isStale` retries it on the two-minute backoff used for sources that never landed, and a refresh of the due sources always includes it, so ordinary use of the app keeps it filling. The daily job and the server's start-up refresh run it too. A failed run does not count as pending work and waits the hour like any failure.

**Time to cover.** One city is 24 requests and about 24 s at one request a second (start spacing, two in flight). On Vercel the 45 s budget (plus the 10 s a running source may use) fits two cities when each run starts at once, so about 2 per run. The owner's places: 24 s each, so 10 minutes for 25 places; the number of places in the real store is not known here. The top 500 cities: 500 x 24.4 s = 3.4 hours of requests, which is about 250 Vercel runs. A run can start every two minutes while someone uses the app, so 8 to 9 hours of continuous use; on the daily cron alone it is 2 cities a day, about 250 days, which is too slow, and a long-running server does better (60 cities, 24 minutes, per daily run and per start, back to back while the app is used: 3.4 hours of use, or about 9 days idle). The daily job does not raise the limit on Vercel; the owner's places go first so they are covered first on any host.

**State and size.** Per checked city the store keeps the time and at most 30 highlights as the taxon id, common name, iconic taxon and twelve monthly counts (about 2.4 KB; 48 cities were 114 KB). Season, share and title are worked out again on reading. 500 cities are about 1.2 MB and 5,000 about 12 MB in `events-live.json`, rewritten at each save. State written by the earlier phenomenon list has another shape and is discarded.

**Real run.** 48 cities (the places named in the owner's brief that exist in the city dataset, and others over every continent), 24 requests and 24 s each. See the pull request for the per-city results. Cherry blossom came out for Kyoto (Japanese Cherry, Mar to Apr, 83% of 171 observations) and was a stored candidate for Nagoya (Mar to Apr) but not for Tokyo, where the flowering cherries are outnumbered by commoner spring plants such as long-headed poppy and philadelphia fleabane. Washington, Amsterdam, Avignon, Pretoria, Lisbon, Cape Town and Monterey did not show cherries, tulips, lavender, jacaranda or whales among their three. Blue jacaranda was a stored candidate in Mexico City, Morelia, Buenos Aires and Kathmandu, and humpback whales came out for Honolulu. With 48 cities checked the share rule removed two species (Asian Lady Beetle, a highlight in 9 cities, and red deadnettle in 8) and changed 34 of the 138 emitted slots.

### climate-seasons

Computed at read time from each city's monthly normals (`seasonality.ts`: mean temperature and precipitation by month), never fetched and never stored. One rule for every city that has twelve months of normals:

- **Rainy season**: months with at least 1.8 times the city's monthly mean precipitation and at least 120 mm.
- **Dry season**: only where a rainy season exists, months at or under 0.3 times the monthly mean.
- **Snow season**: months with a mean temperature of 0 C or lower and at least 20 mm of precipitation.

Each is the longest run of consecutive months (wrapping the year; the wetter or drier run on a tie), kind `season`, recurring, tagged `rainy-season`, `dry-season` or `snow-season` and `climate`, with a note that gives the rule. Like tourism seasons they are left off the map by default and show in a city's list. In the committed data 134 cities have monthly normals (the rest are fetched on demand and by the nightly job, so the number grows): 51 have a rainy season, 48 a dry season and 16 a snow season, 115 events in all.

### aurora

Computed: a city's geomagnetic latitude comes from the centred dipole formula with the pole at 80.7 N, 72.7 W. Cities at 62 degrees or more share one "Northern lights season" event over September to March (142 cities in the dataset); cities at -62 or less would get a southern one over March to September (none do). Live: the NOAA SWPC planetary K-index forecast lists Kp per three hours; when the highest predicted or estimated value in the next 72 hours reaches 5, a dated "Aurora alert (Kp N)" goes on cities at 55 degrees or more geomagnetic latitude (north or south). If SWPC is unreachable the seasons stay and the alert is missing until the next run, three hours later.

### tourism-seasons

Derived at read time from Meridian's own data, never fetched and never stored (up to two events for each of a few hundred cities would add 1 MB of rewrites to every save). For a city with measured monthly visitor nights (ADR 0013, `measuredBusyness`), one recurring "Peak season" over the months at 80 or more of 100. Where the city's Comfort Index curve is cached, one "Shoulder season" over the months next to the peak with a Comfort Index of 60 or more. A city with only the comfort-curve crowd proxy gets nothing. The first run gave 643 cities with measured data and 668 events (25 shoulder seasons: only cities whose climate is already cached qualify). The result is kept for an hour. Status shows the counts.

## Kinds and months

`MeridianEvent.kind` is `festival`, `eclipse`, `sky`, `nature` or `season`; `sport`, `culture` and `other` stay in the type for stored data from the first version. `months` (optional, in calendar order from `month`) holds the months of a window, and `note` one line of detail. `month` stays the first month, so older readers work. The map month filter, Replay's month matching and the trip season view match any of `months`. Tourism and climate seasons are left out of the map's points by default (`GET /api/overlays/events?month=N` takes `seasons=1`): a peak season for 640 cities would bury the markers. Kind `season` is labelled "Season" and covers the tourism seasons and the rainy, dry and snow seasons. They stay in a city's list (`?cityIds=`), trip seasons and the tag chips. Markers are coloured by kind and the map shows a key.

## Placing and caching

`events-live.json` in the document store: `{ version, fetchedAt, attemptedAt, sources, events, state }`, one status line per source (`ok`, `count`, `unmatched`, `unplaced`, `detail`, `error`, `fetchedAt`). Events carry their `source` (`wikidata`, `inaturalist`, `noaa-swpc`, `meridian`) and a `sourceUrl`, and, inside the file only, the feed that produced them. `state` holds the iNaturalist highlights found per city. The file name is unchanged from the first version.

- `refreshLiveEvents` runs the sources and writes the store. The daily job `events` (`server/lib/eventsJob.ts`) calls it for every source, on the in-process scheduler and on Vercel's cron alike.
- `getLiveEvents` is what Replay, Watches, the map overlay and the trip season view call. It reads the store, adds the computed events, and, when a source is due (never landed, or older than its own interval), starts one refresh of the due sources, shared by concurrent callers, serving the old copy meanwhile; only an empty store waits. After an attempt it does not start another for an hour (two minutes while some source has never landed). The server also calls it once at start.
- Consumers keep their rules: recurring events always, dated ones only when still ahead (and, for Replay, only in a month's next occurrence).

## Etiquette

Wikidata: two queries at a time, a second between sources in each lane, `User-Agent: Meridian/2.0 (personal travel planner; https://github.com/kygura/meridian)` and `Accept: application/sparql-results+json`. A 429 or 503 waits `Retry-After` (at most 60 s, 5 s steps when absent) and retries at most twice, then gives up on that source; the request timeout is Wikidata's own 60 s. iNaturalist: request starts one second apart with two in flight, the same User-Agent, at most 60 cities a run, a 429 waits `Retry-After` (10 s when absent, at most 30 s) and retries the request twice for every request in flight before the run stops and keeps what was saved. One 429 came in about 870 requests of the real run, before the retry existed. SWPC: one small request every 3 hours. Tests never reach the network (they inject a fetch with recorded responses; without one the code refuses to run under Vitest, and `LIVE_EVENTS=off` turns fetching off anywhere).

## Failure

A source that fails (an error, a timeout, a 429 that persists, or no events where it had events before and it is not allowed to be empty) keeps its previous events and its previous `fetchedAt`, and records the error. A rolling source keeps what it saved before the failure. The aurora and nature sources may legitimately return no events; the aurora seasons are computed, so only the live alert is missing when SWPC is down. If every source fails, the store and `fetchedAt` stay as they were. `getLiveEvents` never throws: it returns what it has, or an empty list, and the failure is logged.

## Endpoints

`GET /api/overlays/events` (by month or by cities) carries `asOf` (the fetch time) and `source` (the list of sources). `GET /api/overlays/events/tags` lists the distinct tags of upcoming events with counts; Replay's "Happening while you're there" chips load from it, now with tags such as `cherry-blossom`, `peak-season`, `fireflies` and `aurora`. `GET /api/overlays/events/status` shows each source's count, detail, errors and fetch time, with no secrets.

## Serverless time limits

A Vercel function stops at 60 s. A refresh saves each source as soon as it finishes (a rolling source after each city), runs two at a time, and on Vercel stops starting sources and checks after a 45 s budget; the rest is the oldest-first work of the next run. A request that finds the store empty waits at most 8 s, then answers with what has been saved; the rest keeps running through the request's `waitUntil`. A source already running may use 10 s past the budget. Requests refresh only the sources that are due; the daily job refreshes every source. The Wikidata queries took 31 s over five queries in the last full run (the solar eclipse query was the slowest at 17 s), SWPC 0.2 s. A highlights check is 24 requests and about 24 s, so on Vercel the iNaturalist lane checks about two cities and starts none that cannot finish before the limit; it never starts a city it would have to abandon.

## Known limits

Wikidata's notability bias remains: well-known festivals appear and small local ones are mostly absent. A "festival" that Wikidata gives no film or music type can pass the filters. Moving festivals have no month in Wikidata and are read as described above; Easter-relative, Thai lunar and Islamic ones are not covered. The national filter still lets some observances through. Few lunar eclipses are classed as total. Observations on iNaturalist show where people look and what gets photographed, so a season there is a measured tendency, not a forecast, and a species that is common in the data is not necessarily one worth travelling for: in the real run weeds such as philadelphia fleabane outranked Tokyo's cherries, and a city with few observers (Hanoi, Lagos, Singapore) has few or no highlights. Observation counts are uneven across the world. "In bloom" for plants is inferred from when a plant is photographed. Climate seasons exist only for cities with monthly normals (134 in the committed data). Tourism seasons come from nights in tourist accommodation at region or country level, not from the city. Tags are whatever Wikidata's type labels say, plus the tags above.

Rejected: keeping the hand-written list and refreshing it by hand (it is the problem this ADR removes); PredictHQ and Ticketmaster (paid or keyed, and they cover ticketed shows and concerts rather than seasonal events); scraping tourism sites (licences, and brittle); a fixed list of cherry blossom or whale places, or of species to look up (an event list in the code in another form); a source per country such as the Japan Meteorological Agency's phenology tables (it cannot be applied to the rest of the world); asking the model for events at request time (no sources, not repeatable, and a fabricated date is worse than a missing one).
