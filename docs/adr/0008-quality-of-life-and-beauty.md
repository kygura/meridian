# Quality of life and beauty are measured, decomposed and shown with their parts

The map's old "Quality of life" surface (and the `quality` part of the city score) was a hash of the city's name. It is replaced by measured data, and beauty is added as a second, separate measure. Both are built offline by `scripts/build-livability.ts` into `data/livability.json`, like the seasonality dataset.

**Quality of life** (0–100) is how good a city is to live in: Human Development Index (30%), life satisfaction from the World Happiness Report (25%), safety from the homicide rate (20%) and air quality as PM2.5 (25%). A city needs a development or happiness figure and two parts in all to get a score (territories with only a homicide rate don't). The first three are national; air is measured at the city for major cities (capitals, large hubs, 5M+) from Open-Meteo/CAMS, and is the national mean elsewhere. `local` records how much of the score is truly local, and the UI says so.

**Beauty** (0–100) combines **natural setting** and **built character**, kept as separate axes because a mountain village and a baroque capital are beautiful for different reasons; the stronger axis counts 65% and the weaker 35%, so excelling at one (Queenstown, Venice) isn't halved by the other. Setting combines terrain relief within 15 km (on dry land) and water (sea from bathymetry 20 m or more deep, lakes from Natural Earth) as either/or: mountains alone or water alone make a setting, both more so. Water is capped below mountains (sea 40–70, lakes 40–55), so a flat coastline is pleasant rather than spectacular. Shallow lagoons and harbours (Venice, Hong Kong) are missed, because elevation alone can't tell them from land below sea level. Character combines distance to UNESCO World Heritage sites (40%), the density of geotagged Commons photos at the centre (35%) and notable places on Wikipedia within 5 km (25%). Photo counts are re-measured at 500 m where 2 km fills the API's 500-result cap, so busy centres still rank against each other.

Every part is stored and shown: the city panel lists the parts and the plain facts behind them ("1,820 m of relief within 15 km", "fine particles 7.2 µg/m³, measured here"). Plan › Live & love plots quality against beauty (or either beauty axis) with a ranked "best of both" table.

Known biases, accepted for v1: character favours famous places (photographed and written about), which is why setting is half the score and photos are a density, not a total; quality is mostly national, so cities within a country differ mainly by air. Rejected: a single opaque "liveability" number (hides why), crowd-sourced cost-of-living and safety indexes (licence and coverage), and scraping review sites (terms and bias). Next candidates: greenery (NDVI), transit and walkability (OpenStreetMap), and climate comfort across the year once every city has seasonality data.
