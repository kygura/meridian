# Locales are researched places to live, scored from Meridian's own data, with their own calendar

Builds on ADR 0011 (Rotation, Personal Windows, Fit) and ADR 0017 (the Notion bridge). Leaves both as they are.

The owner researches where to live before any stay exists: a scored list of bases, hubs and one-off destinations with the months to be there, how long a place holds up, and how to get in. A Rotation Stay is a commitment with dates; a Place Profile is the owner's own months for one city; the Notion pool (ADR 0017) is candidates for the solver. None of them can say "Da Nang / Hoi An is a base, best February to May, two to three months, on a 90-day e-visa". So a **Locale** is its own primitive.

**Model** (`src/lib/locales.ts`, pure; data in `data/locales.json`). A locale has an id, a name, a country, a region, a role (`base`, `hub` or `destination`) and one or more City ids, the first being the anchor whose Comfort Index and place data stand for the locale. Twelve researched month ratings (`best`, `good`, `fair`, `avoid`) with short reasons, a stay range in weeks (minimum, ideal, maximum, with why), an all-in cost range as written (EUR per month, or per trip for a destination), the owner's own 1–10 scores where the research has them, an asymmetry rank, the long-stay entry route with a source link and the month it was checked, timing-worthy events, the airport, an optional booking lead time, and corrections where the review found the owner's notes wrong. Where the place has no city in the dataset (Kotor, Saranda, Chania, Polignano), the nearest one stands in and `area` says so.

**Months come from Meridian's own data.** Each month reads the owner's Personal Window for the anchor city first (ADR 0011: the owner's word inside Meridian), else the anchor city's Comfort Index (ADR 0002, 0014, 0018: temperature, rain, air, desert), binned as the rotation bins it (`comfortFit`). A seasonal hazard in reach lowers the month: the map's anomalies with months (`data/phenomena.json`), scored as trip risk scores a place (severity × `proximity` of distance); at "watch" (0.75) the month drops one level, at "caution" (1.75) it becomes avoid. Tourist season per month comes from Meridian's computed tourism seasons (peak and shoulder from measured visitor nights, ADR 0021); where a city is not measured there is none. The owner's notes (their Travel Seasons window covering at least half the month, else the reviewed research rating) ride along on every month, unscored, and a month where they read two or more levels away is flagged in the sheet. The research cannot score because it is a reading of the same weather with less data behind it, and two scores for one month would leave the calendar arguing with itself; where it knows something Meridian does not, the fix is a hazard in `phenomena.json` or a rotation window, not an override here. Weekly normals, where the anchor city has them, are drawn at week resolution; planning stays monthly.

**Ideal staying windows** (`stayWindows`). A window is a run of great or good months, wrapping the new year; else a run of fair months (marked); and, for a place in Meridian's top 15 % for Beauty that is never better than fair, its most tolerable months, within 10 points of its peak, when longer ("worth it", drawn dashed; ADR 0023). Its weeks are the locale's stay range cut to the run's length and, while signed in, to the day limit of the owner's best passport for that country (`visa/data.cellOf`; free movement and the home country have none). The stay itself is the ideal length in whole months, placed on the stretch of the run with the most great months, then the fewest tourist-peak months, then the soonest start. Windows are ranked by great months, then run length. Rolling limits such as Schengen 90/180 are the Days page's job and are only pointed to.

**Page** (`#/locales`, public). A rolling twelve-month timeline from this month. Each row is one continuous strip of the anchor city's fit (weekly where possible), with the ideal stay as a bracket around its months, a line for today, seasonal hazards and live events (festivals, highlights, sky) as marks above, and tourist peaks as a thin lane below. Nothing else is drawn on the strip. The header sticks while the rows scroll; hovering a month shades its column, clicking a month heading ranks every locale by that month; the whole row opens the locale and takes the keyboard. On a phone the timeline scrolls sideways under a pinned locale column. Rows group by region, or sort by fit in a month, by Meridian score (the mean of quality of life, safety, healthcare, affordability and internet surfaces) or by affordability. Below it, the top three bases for each month. A sheet per locale gives the windows, every month with its comfort, hazard, crowds and the owner's note, the metric surfaces, live events, entry, and then "Your notes" (Travel Seasons, research summary, cost band, scores, corrections) as context. While signed in, "Add to rotation" writes an idea stay for the next time the window comes round through the rotation's existing stay API, with the window's nights, the research's upper cost as budget and the locale's lead time; ideas never count as commitments (ADR 0011).

**Where the data came from.** The owner's Notion database "Travel Seasons" (45 windows over 20 locations; "Montenegro / Albania" applies to both locales, "Valencia / Malaga" adds Málaga to Valencia, and Seoul and Sri Lanka East became locales of their own) and the Notion page "Research on Locations" (scores and sources there: Numbeo 2026, CEOWORLD 2025, OAG 2025, TasteAtlas 25/26, EIU, Global Peace Index), reviewed in November 2026 for monsoon, typhoon, burning-season and air-quality timing and for current entry rules. The month ratings are authored data with reasons, not parsed prose, which keeps ADR 0017's rule: the Notion importer still does not turn "Best:" and "Avoid:" lines into windows. Corrections are kept on the locale, not silently applied to the owner's notes. Entry rules change; each carries its checked month, and Settings › Visas remains the source for day counting.

Rejected: Locales as Place Profiles (a profile is per city and the owner's own; locales span cities and are research the owner may disagree with); as solver pool entries (the pool is the solver's input; a locale can feed it later through `stayFromLocale`); storing them server-side per owner (shared research with no edits yet, shipped like the other datasets; a per-owner override belongs in Personal Windows); scoring months from the research or the Travel Seasons (a second, hand-written reading of the weather that disagrees with the index and cannot be recomputed; tried first and dropped); and rescaling the Comfort Index per place so the tropics look comfortable (colours would stop comparing places; the "worth it" window answers when to go without pretending; ADR 0023 eased the index itself instead).
